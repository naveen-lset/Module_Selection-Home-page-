The module launcher · Option 4, revised

Option 4 with your four changes. Not full page — the field is a 636×398 block, 62% of the width and 52% of the height, where it was 912px wide before. A white Apple material instead of the black frost: white at 62% over a 30px blur, which brightens and desaturates the page rather than darkening it. Fixed colours only — the gradients are gone, and each module's flat colour is derived from its own ramp at the 50% point rather than picked by eye. The fan you liked is unchanged: every tile still flies out of the pill along its own measured vector.

Two variants, because two of your instructions pull against each other. "Not full page" and "white blurred background" disagree about how far the white should travel — 4A keeps the material as a panel around the block, 4B keeps it page-wide with the small block floating on it. The tiles, the colours and the animation are identical in both. Press Replay, or scroll one into view.

The 8 September brief does not govern this component. That brief was about a panel of sixteen verbs — one restrained surface, one neutral wash, no plates, no colour. A launcher of nineteen modules is a different thing doing a different job, so those rules are not so much broken here as left behind. But it leaves a real question open: if this replaces the verb panel, the sixteen verbs need somewhere to live. See the last note at the foot.

A white label on these gradients fails AA on 14 of the 19 — Parivesh 1.60:1, Lab 1.83, Eggs 1.87. Toggle to compare; it applies to all six at once.

  • 3 and 5 are the two I would put in front of you. Both answer "everything is the same container" with a genuinely different shape rather than a restyle, and both leave the page readable — which was the whole argument behind the 8 September backdrop rule, and is still true whatever the frost is set to today.
  • 6 is the honest continuation of what already ships and the one I would build if the answer is "keep the current feel, change the contents". It reuses the existing premise — the control transforms into the surface — so the motion work already done is not thrown away. Its cost is real though: width and padding keyframes drive layout on every frame where the live panel animates clip-path, which does not.
  • The one decision left inside Option 4 is where the colour lives. On the tile, as drawn, the nineteen fixed colours are unmistakable but five labels land between 3.88 and 4.31:1 — over the 3:1 floor, under the 4.5 a label wants. On the icon chip, with the label on a neutral wash, the worst case is 13.20:1 and every module keeps its colour. The toggle at the top of this page switches between them; nothing else about the concept changes.
  • THE OPEN QUESTION, and it is bigger than the container. The sixteen verbs — + Site, + Note, Dispense Medicine, Transfer Animal — are what the pill opens today, and every concept here replaces them with modules. Those verbs still need somewhere to live. Three possibilities, none of them free: the verbs move one level down, so a module tile opens its own verbs (two taps, and the launcher becomes a menu); the verbs move to the avatar menu, which already claims to hold everything a person can do; or the launcher and the verb panel are two different controls, which means a second FAB. Worth settling before any of these is built, because it decides whether a module tile navigates or expands.
