Quick Actions, ten approaches

The sixteen verbs of the home page's Quick Actions, drawn ten ways and every one of them inside a 1024×768 iPad in landscape — at real size, over the page it opens on top of. Nothing is scaled, so what the page compares is how much of the screen each approach uses and what it does with the room.

A is the build, and it is the control. What is wrong with it is not that it is ugly:

  • 1No hierarchy. Sixteen cells at one size claim the sixteen verbs are equally likely. “+ Note” is an every-shift action; “+ Site” happens when the estate grows.
  • 2No grouping. Site, Section and Enclosure are one subject; Accession, Transfer and Missing/Escaped are another. The grid interleaves them, so finding one means reading all sixteen.
  • 3The label is the whole explanation. “Master” and “Dispense Medicine” each need a second line to say what they do, and at 12px in a 108px cell there is no room for one.
  • 4It spends 456×427 of a 1024×768 screen and still looks cramped — phone-sized cells on a tablet.

The verbs and their order are js/data/quickActions.js verbatim and the glyphs are lifted from js/icons.js. The one-line descriptions in D, F, H and I are the only invented copy here — they are what the app would have to write if it chose one of those.

What to look at, and what I would pick

  • Grouping is the whole game. Every variation that groups (B, D, E, G, H, J) can be scanned by subject in one jump; A, C and F still ask you to read a list. The four groups are the estate, the animals, their care, and the office — and they are not my invention, they are how the modules are already divided.
  • Where the four verbs without a “+” go. Master, Transfer Animal, Dispense Medicine and Announcement act on records that already exist. E is built entirely on that line and is the only one that makes it visible; everywhere else they sit among the twelve as if they created something.
  • How much screen is used. A takes 23% of the iPad, F 45%, G and J about 70%. More is not better on its own — but a 456px panel on a 1024px screen is the specific complaint that started this page, and B, D, G, H and J all answer it.
  • What each one costs to build. B, E, F and J are the same markup the build already has with different CSS — a day. C needs a filter and keyboard handling. D and H need state (which group is showing). G changes where the surface lives.
  • My pick: B, with E's split inside it. Four labelled columns fill the width without inventing a hierarchy nobody has measured, every verb keeps a readable label, and the “act on” four sit under their own heading instead of pretending to be creates. D is the one to choose instead if the sixteen will become thirty — a rail scales where four columns do not. I is the one I would not choose yet: it ranks the six most likely, and nobody has the data to say which six those are.
