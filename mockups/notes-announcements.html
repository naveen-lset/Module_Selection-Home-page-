Notes & Announcements, ten ways

The two widgets from the design file, drawn ten ways in the home grid's 2×2 footprint — 374×344. Every variation is that same box for both widgets, side by side, so the only thing that changes down the page is the composition. Copy is the app's own seed copy from observations.js and homeBanners.js; colour, photographs and icons are the app's.

A is the design file refitted, and it is the control: the announcement's 32px gradient title and 365px photograph were drawn for a 952px banner, and a third of that width is what the other nine are answers to.

What to look at, and what I would pick

  • How much copy actually survives. Every widget here is filled with the same full-length seed note and clamped to what its own box holds, so the count is the finding: B and H give the note six lines, C, I and J five, A’s narrow column eight short ones, D four under its picture, E three per item across two items, G a title and nothing else, F no prose at all across four items. That trade is the whole decision.
  • Where the colour is. A and J put the priority at full strength behind the text, which is loud beside eleven module cards; C moves it into a band; B and E cut it to an edge or a rule; F to a dot. The priority is never carried by colour alone anywhere here — the badge, the word or the eyebrow says it too.
  • Whether the two widgets look like one system. A does not: the Notes card is filled and the announcement is tinted, so the pair reads as two products. Every other variation gives both the same treatment and only the hue differs.
  • The photograph. At 374px a picture beside the copy leaves a 214px column of eight-word lines (A), and full bleed costs the body altogether (G). D's 16:9 top is the only place a picture and a paragraph both fit — and half the observations have no picture, so it fills that box with the priority colour instead, exactly as the rail's card does.
  • My pick: D for Notes, C or B for Announcements — or E for both if the home page should show two items rather than one. D keeps the observation card the rail already uses, which means one card component and no second design to maintain; C gives the announcement its name and its tone without spending the content area on either.
